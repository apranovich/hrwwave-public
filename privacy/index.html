Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 November 2026

HRVWave is a breathing app made by an independent developer ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the app keeps on your phone, what it sends, and what it never collects. If you have a question, write to hrvwave.app@gmail.com.

The short version

  • Your heartbeats, heart rate and recordings stay on your phone. We never receive them.
  • There is no account. We never ask for your name, email, phone number or location.
  • No ads, no advertising identifier, and no tracking across other companies' apps or websites.
  • The app sends anonymous usage events to Google Firebase Analytics, so we can see which parts of the app are used and fix what doesn't work.
  • Only if you allow it, the app also sends your HRV, coherence and pace test results. It asks once, after your first session, and you can change your answer in Settings > Privacy.

What stays on your phone

Everything you measure and do in the app is stored only on your iPhone: your heart data from the device you connect (beat-to-beat intervals and heart rate), session recordings, pace test results, your history and trends, and your settings and reminders. We have no copy of any of it.

Like other app data, it can be included in your iPhone backups (iCloud or a computer), which Apple handles under its own privacy policy. Deleting a session or test in the app removes it from your phone. Deleting the app removes all of it.

When you use the share button on a session or test, the app creates a file and hands it to the app you choose (for example Mail or Files). From then on, the file is handled by that app and by the people you send it to.

Bluetooth and notifications

The app uses Bluetooth only to connect to your heart rate device and read its data. That data is processed on your phone.

Reminders are scheduled by your phone. Nothing about them is sent to us.

What the app sends: anonymous usage analytics

The app uses Google Firebase Analytics. It records events such as:

  • which screens you open and which buttons you tap;
  • which settings you change, for example the session length, sound, vibration and reminders;
  • how pace tests and sessions go, for example how long a session lasted, which pace you used and whether a test found a pace (but not the pace it found, see below);
  • which kind of device you connect (chest strap, optical sensor or heart rate only), but never its name or ID;
  • how you move through the subscription screen: which plan you looked at, started a trial for or bought, but never a price, receipt or payment detail;
  • the app's language and whether you have HRVWave Pro.

These events are linked to a random identifier that Firebase creates for each installation of the app. It is not your Apple ID, not the advertising identifier and not a device serial number, and it changes if you delete and reinstall the app. Firebase sees your IP address when the app sends events and uses it to estimate your country and city. Google states that Google Analytics doesn't log or store IP addresses.

Session numbers (only with your permission)

After your first session with a heart rate device, the app asks whether you want to share your session numbers. Until you say yes, they aren't sent. If you allow it ("Share anonymous session numbers" in Settings > Privacy), the app sends:

  • after each session: your HRV before the session (if you took a resting snapshot) and during it, in milliseconds, and your median coherence, in percent;
  • after each pace test: the pace it found, in breaths a minute.

These help us understand whether the practice works for people. You can switch it off at any time in Settings > Privacy. Then these numbers stop being sent, and everything else in the app works the same.

What we never collect

Your heartbeats or heart rate, any series of measurements, recording files, device names or IDs, your name, email, phone number, contacts, photos, location, or any text you type.

Who processes the data

Google processes the analytics data for us as a service provider, on servers that may be outside your country, including in the United States. See Google's information on how Firebase handles data and the Google Privacy Policy.

Payments are handled entirely by Apple. We never see your payment details or your Apple ID. Apple tells the app which plan you have, so it can unlock HRVWave Pro on your phone. Apple also gives us sales reports that don't identify you.

We don't sell data, share it with advertisers or use it to make decisions about you.

How long the data is kept

Event-level analytics data is kept for no longer than 14 months and then deleted by Google. Totals that don't relate to any installation (for example, "1,000 sessions this week") may be kept longer.

Why we use the data

We use analytics to fix problems and improve the app. If you live in the European Economic Area or the UK: we rely on our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and improving it for the usage events, and on your explicit consent for the session numbers. You can withdraw it at any time in Settings > Privacy.

Your choices and rights

  • Switch off "Share anonymous session numbers" in Settings > Privacy at any time.
  • Delete sessions and tests in the app, or delete the app to remove everything on your phone. Reinstalling starts a new random identifier.
  • You can ask us to access or delete data, or object to its use, by writing to hrvwave.app@gmail.com. Because the data isn't linked to your name or email, we usually can't find what belongs to you, but we'll help as far as we can. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

HRVWave isn't meant for children under 16, and we don't knowingly collect data from them.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top and the copy in the app. If a change affects what is sent from your phone, we'll say so in the app's update notes.

What is resonance breathing

Resonance breathing means breathing slowly at the pace that suits your body best. According to studies, doing it regularly helps you:

  • calm down right after a session
  • sleep better
  • handle stress better
  • raise your HRV over the weeks

Why your pace matters

When you inhale, your heart speeds up a little. When you exhale, it slows down. So your heart rate goes up and down like a wave. The bigger your wave, the better your body switches from stress to rest. Your HRV, heart rate variability, shows how big that wave is.

At one pace, called your resonance breathing frequency, the wave is at its biggest. For most people it's 4.5 to 6.5 breaths a minute, and it's a bit different for everyone.

The wave comes from your vagus nerve, the nerve that calms you down. The bigger the wave, the harder it's working. So each session is a workout for your nervous system. Keep at it, and your body gets quicker at relaxing when stress hits.

How HRVWave finds your pace

You sit for 13 minutes and breathe with the app at 6 slow paces, 2 minutes each. The app measures your heart wave at each one. The pace with the biggest wave is yours.

Wear a heart rate chest strap for the test. A watch or band works too, but it's less exact. From time to time, a quick 7-minute check fine-tunes your pace.

Based on research: Lehrer, Vaschillo & Vaschillo, 2000; Shaffer & Meehan, 2020; Capdevila et al., 2021
